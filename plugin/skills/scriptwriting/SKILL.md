# Scriptwriting

A script is a sequence of promises and payoffs. Every line either makes the
viewer want the next one or delivers on an earlier one. Cut anything that
does neither.

## 1 · The hook (first 1–3 seconds)

Pick one device; never "Hey guys", "In this video", or the product name first.

| Device | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Contrarian | "X is wrong." | "Your onboarding email is why people churn." |
| Specific number | a precise, surprising figure | "We cut CI from 4 minutes to 6 seconds." |
| Enemy | name the pain everyone shares | "Three boxes. Forty minutes. One arrow that won't sit." |
| Before → after | the transformation in one breath | "Standup: 15 minutes. This one: 30 seconds." |
| Open question | a question the viewer must answer | "Which diagram answers *which* question?" |
| Show first | the payoff on screen before any words | (visual hook; first line names what they just saw) |

The hook line and the first visual must say the same thing.

## 2 · Structures by length

Speech runs ~2.4 words/second (≈150 wpm). Budget words before writing.

**15 s (≈ 35 words) — Short:** hook → one proof → payoff/CTA. Loopable: the
last line should flow back into the first.

**30 s (≈ 70 words) — Reel / launch film:** hook (3 s) → problem in one
concrete image (5 s) → the turn: what changes (6 s) → two proofs (10 s) →
payoff + CTA (6 s).

**60 s (≈ 140 words) — Explainer:** hook → why it matters to *them* → how it
works in three steps → one proof → what it means for them → CTA.

**3–10 min — YouTube:** cold open (≤ 30 s: prove the title, why it matters,
what they'll get) → chapters, each with a mini-hook and an open loop into the
next → a re-hook every 60–90 s → the biggest moment at 50–70 % → payoff →
CTA after the payoff.

**45–120 s — Short film:** use a story spine (below), product as the turning
point.

## 3 · Story spines

- **Pixar spine:** Once there was… Every day… Until one day… Because of that…
  Because of that… Until finally… Ever since then…
- **Problem → Agitate → Solve:** name it, make it hurt (specific, small,
  real), then the relief.
- **Before → After → Bridge:** today, the better day, the bridge (product).
- **Hero's turn (for founders):** the moment, the weight, the turn, after,
  close (mirror the opening line).

## 4 · Holding attention

- **Open loops:** raise a question you answer later ("…but that only works
  if one thing is true"). Close every loop you open.
- **Specificity beats adjectives:** "40 minutes nudging arrows" > "time-consuming".
- **Pattern interrupts:** change rhythm every 2–4 beats — a one-word line, a
  number, a question, a silence (a beat with no voice).
- **Re-hook** in long videos every 60–90 s: "Here's where it gets weird."
- **Payoff the promise** the hook made, explicitly.

## 5 · Writing for the ear

- One idea per line; 4–14 words; contractions; active voice.
- Read it aloud in your head. If you'd run out of breath, split it.
- Numbers as people say them ("thirty seconds", "a hundred and thirty thousand").
- Spell brand names exactly as the brief does; avoid acronyms you'd never say.
- No hype words (revolutionary, seamless, unlock, game-changer, supercharge).
- The voice line and the on-screen text say the same idea, not the same words.

## 6 · Before you hand it over

- [ ] Hook ≤ 3 s, specific, matches the first visual
- [ ] Word count fits the length budget
- [ ] Every line earns the next (cut the ones that don't)
- [ ] Every open loop is closed; the hook's promise is paid off
- [ ] One CTA, after the payoff
- [ ] Reads aloud without stumbling
